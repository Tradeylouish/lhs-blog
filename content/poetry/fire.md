The poets say desire is like a flame  
Though none alive have truly known the fire  
One may be burnt but that is not the same  
As flesh begetting bones upon a pyre  

And yet I can't deny the heat that glows  
Inside as embers might still cling to life  
If wood and breath stoke them it only grows  
A sudden fear cuts through me like a knife  

I could quench them with water used to drown  
And leave the ash to mark the resting place  
Safe from devouring blaze that I deep down  
Am sure as hell I'd quickly come to face  

But I know back to ancient times this harks  
And so I sit and contemplate the sparks  

---

*Inspired by Sappho [31](https://digitalsappho.org/fragments/fr31/) and [48](https://digitalsappho.org/fragments/fr48/) (Diane Rayor's [translation](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/63827321-sappho) recommended), which associate πῦρ (fire) and καίω (to burn) with πτοέω (to be excited by passion) and καίω (desire). I think it's cool that this metaphoric tradition continues in English poetry like [Fire and Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_and_Ice_(poem)). Now we can even rhyme fire / desire, a valuable linguistic innovation.*
